FREE SAMPLE LESSON · SECURITY PRINCIPLES

Least privilege:
access by need.

Give each person, device, or process only the access it needs to do its job—and no more.

Why it matters

An account with broad permissions can do more damage if it is misused or compromised. Least privilege limits that potential impact. It also makes unusual access easier to spot.

Put it into practice

Suppose an employee needs to view invoices but not change vendor bank details. Give them read access to invoices and withhold payment-management permissions. If their duties change, review and adjust access rather than leaving old permissions in place.

Remember: Least privilege applies to people and to systems. A service account should not automatically run with administrator rights just because that is convenient.

Related ideas

  • Need to know: restrict access to information relevant to a task.
  • Separation of duties: divide sensitive steps among people or roles to reduce mistakes and abuse.
  • Privileged access management: control, monitor, and review powerful accounts.

Quick check

A reporting tool only needs to read records. Should its account also be allowed to delete them?

Reveal the answer

No. Read-only access is the least-privilege choice when deletion is not part of the tool’s job.
